\begin{abstract}
An integer Heffter array set $\IHS(m,3;c)$ consists of $c$ fully filled $m\times3$ arrays
with zero row and column sums, using every absolute value in $[1,3mc]$ exactly once.
We construct $\IHS(m,3;3)$ for every $m\ge7$ with $m\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\gcd(m,3)=1$, and
$\IHS(m,3;4)$ for every odd $m\ge5$ with $\gcd(m,3)=1$.
These results settle the $c=3$ and $c=4$ slices of the coprime odd-row existence problem.
Starting from centre-complementary Kotzig arrays, we use bounded corrections, encoded by
offset words of lengths $24$ and $16$, to construct zero-sum rows with the required
absolute-value support. A residue-class grouping assigns the rows to arrays, and an exact
orientation criterion reduces column balance to two independent signed-sum identities.
For each fixed parameter phase, support is certified by affine endpoint identities, while
the signed balance totals are polynomials of degree at most two in the block parameter after
all absolute-value breakpoints have been resolved. A finite-certificate transport lemma extends these verifications to the infinite
parameter ranges, and explicit constructions cover every earlier admissible value.
All finite construction data are included in the paper.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer Heffter array; Heffter array set; Skolem sequence; Kotzig array;
finite certificates.\\

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Heffter arrays were introduced by Archdeacon~\cite{Arch2015} in the study of orientable
biembeddings of complete graphs.  Their existence theory asks whether elementary counting and
parity conditions suffice to arrange a prescribed signed support with zero row and column sums.
Pasotti and Dinitz~\cite{PasottiDinitz2024} survey this theory.  Reduction and relative-array
constructions appear in~\cite{MoriniPellegrini2020,MoriniPellegrini2022}.

\begin{definition}
An integer Heffter array $\Hm(m,n;s,k)$ is an $m\times n$ partially filled array, with entries from
$\{\pm1,\pm2,\dots,\pm nk\}\subset\Z$, such that each row has exactly $s$ filled cells and each column
exactly $k$; the absolute values of the entries are pairwise distinct; and every row sum and column sum
is $0$.
\end{definition}

For an integer Heffter array $\Hm(m,n;s,k)$, the necessary conditions are
$ms=nk$, $3\le s\le n$, $3\le k\le m$, and $nk\equiv0,3\pmod4$; Archdeacon's conjecture
asserts that these conditions are sufficient. In the coprime odd-width regime, Pellegrini and
Traetta~\cite{PT2025} introduced integer Heffter array sets.  The reduction in
Morini and Pellegrini~\cite{MP2025} identifies two short-width construction problems whose
solutions would settle the cases left open by their results.

\begin{definition}
An $\IHS(m,n;c)$ is a set of $c$ \emph{fully filled} arrays of size $m\times n$ whose union has
$mnc$ entries with
pairwise distinct absolute values, collectively equal to $[1,mnc]$. Every row and every column of each
array has sum zero.
\end{definition}

When $s$ and $k$ are coprime, write $m=ck$ and $n=cs$. In this notation, an $\IHS(k,s;c)$
assembles an $\Hm(ck,cs;s,k)$ \cite[proof of Theorem~C]{MP2025}.  This implication is
one-directional: an IHS is a sufficient construction device, not a necessary decomposition of
every Heffter array.  The short-width cases in that reduction have $s=3$ or $s=5$.
We address the following width-three problem from~\cite{MP2025}:

\begin{problem}\label{prob:1}
For every $c\ge1$ and every odd $m\ge3$ with $\gcd(m,3)=1$ and $mc\equiv0,1\pmod4$, construct an
$\IHS(m,3;c)$.
\end{problem}

Throughout this paper, \emph{admissible} refers to the parameter range in
Problem~\ref{prob:1}, including the oddness and coprimality restrictions.
The case $c=1$ is already
settled: an $\IHS(m,3;1)$ is precisely a
\emph{tight} integer Heffter array $\Hm(m,3;3,m)$---an $m\times3$ array with no empty cells whose
support is exactly $[1,3m]$---and Archdeacon, Boothby and Dinitz proved that tight Heffter arrays
exist for all admissible parameters~\cite{ABD2017}; for width three this covers every admissible $m$
(here $m\equiv1\pmod4$, $\gcd(m,3)=1$). The case $c=2$ is vacuous in Problem~\ref{prob:1}, since its
odd parameter $m$ gives $mc=2m\equiv2\pmod4$. Thus the first nonvacuous case beyond one array is
$c=3$, admissible exactly when $3m\equiv1\pmod4$, i.e.\ $m\equiv3\pmod4$. Our first main result
settles it. The next even slice, $c=4$, is admissible for every odd $m$ coprime to $3$; our second
main result settles that slice as well.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $m\ge3$ be odd with $\gcd(m,3)=1$. Then $\IHS(m,3;3)$ exists if and only if $m\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:c4full}
Let $m\ge5$ be odd with $\gcd(m,3)=1$. Then $\IHS(m,3;4)$ exists.
\end{theorem}

Skolem sequences supply a partition of the required support into sum-triples, but do not
prescribe a division into $c$ groups with zero column sums. The additional requirement is
the compatibility of the support partition with these separately balanced groups. We obtain
it from the closed formula for the centre-complementary Kotzig arrays of Zhou, Li, Zhang
and Su~\cite{Zhou2018DM}, using bounded periodic corrections and a congruence-based grouping
rule. An exact orientation criterion then reduces the three column-sum conditions in each
group to two independent signed-sum identities.

The support words have lengths $24$ and $16$ for $c=3$ and $c=4$, respectively; the $c=4$
balance patterns use a $48$-position refinement. Finitely many boundary and orientation
states encode the parameter dependence, and explicit constructions cover the values below
the uniform ranges. Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-transport} supplies the common verification
principle: affine endpoint identities certify support, whereas polynomial identities certify
balance. The polynomial degree bounds are established before finite evaluations are used.

These statements are distinct from the completion of the square relative-array problem
$\mathrm H_k(n;k)$ by Mella~\cite{Mella2026}.  That problem concerns one partially filled
square array with a relative support; here the support $[1,3mc]$ must be divided among $c$
fully filled rectangles. The present paper treats only the $c=3$ and $c=4$ slices of
Problem~\ref{prob:1}. The construction data and bounded cases appear in the main text;
only the $192$-row $c=4$ balance table is placed in the appendix.

\paragraph{Notation.}
$[1,N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$. A \emph{sum-triple} is a set $\{x,y,x+y\}$ of three distinct positive integers.
A multiset is \emph{signable} if some choice of signs makes it sum to $0$ (equivalently, its total is
even and half of it is a subset sum).

\section{Reduction to sum-triples and balanceability}\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:red}
Let $m\ge3$, $c\ge1$, $N=mc$. An $\IHS(m,3;c)$ exists if and only if
\textup{(i)} $[1,3N]$ can be partitioned into $N$ sum-triples, and
\textup{(ii)} these $N$ triples can be split into $c$ groups of $m$, each \emph{orientable} (for each
triple choose a sign and a placement of its three signed values into three columns) so that the three
column sums of the group vanish.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because no entry is zero and the absolute values are distinct, three signed entries in a zero-sum
row can be reordered, and if necessary all negated, as $x$, $y$, and $-(x+y)$, with $x,y>0$.
Thus the absolute values in every
row form a sum-triple. Conversely, every sum-triple $\{x,y,x+y\}$ gives a zero-sum row by using
the signs $(x,y,-(x+y))$. The zero column sums are exactly the orientation condition in (ii), while
the $c$ arrays together cover $[1,3N]$ precisely when the triples form the partition in (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:skolem}
The interval $[1,3N]$ can be partitioned into $N$ sum-triples if and only if
$N\equiv0,1\pmod4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every sum-triple has even total, so necessity is equivalent to the evenness of
$3N(3N+1)/2$, which is exactly $N\equiv0,1\pmod4$. For sufficiency, a Skolem sequence of
order $N$ exists precisely when $N\equiv0,1\pmod4$~\cite{Skolem1957}. Hence there are pairs
$\{x_i,y_i\}$, $i\in[1,N]$, partitioning $[1,2N]$ such that $y_i-x_i=i$. The sets
$\{i,N+x_i,N+y_i\}$ partition $[1,3N]$ and are sum-triples because
$i+(N+x_i)=N+y_i$.
\end{proof}

Thus the support partition exists under exactly the necessary congruence condition. It remains to
balance the triples within each group. Let $C_1,C_2,C_3$ denote the three column sums of a fixed
group. Since every row already sums to zero, $C_1+C_2+C_3=0$; hence zero column sums are
equivalent to the two conditions $C_1+C_2=0$ and $C_1-C_2=0$. Write a sum-triple as
$\{a,b,c\}$ with $a<b$ and $c=a+b$. A legal orientation is a \emph{mode}
$\mathrm m\in\{\mathrm a,\mathrm b,\mathrm c\}$, where the modes $\mathrm a,\mathrm b,\mathrm c$
mean that respectively the smallest, the middle, and the largest element of the triple occupies the
third column; a mode is accompanied by an overall sign and a possible interchange of the first two
columns. Writing $\mu$ for the magnitude of its contribution to $C_1{+}C_2$ and $|w|$ for
that to $C_1{-}C_2$,
\[
(\mathrm m{=}\mathrm c):\ \mu=c,\ |w|=b-a;\quad
(\mathrm m{=}\mathrm a):\ \mu=a,\ |w|=b+c;\quad
(\mathrm m{=}\mathrm b):\ \mu=b,\ |w|=a+c.
\]
These three roman labels are the codes that appear in every switch column of the data tables.
Inside a triple, $c$ denotes its largest element; this local use does not conflict with the number
of arrays $c$, which never appears inside a triple.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:exact}
A group of sum-triples can be oriented to zero column sums if and only if it has a mode assignment
$(\mathrm m_i)_i$ for which both multisets $\{\mu_i\}_i$ and $\{|w_i|\}_i$ are signable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a sign $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$, the three modes may be represented by the signed entries
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{mode}&\text{first two columns}&\text{third column}\\\hline
c&(\varepsilon a,\varepsilon b)&-\varepsilon c\\
a&(\varepsilon c,-\varepsilon b)&-\varepsilon a\\
b&(\varepsilon c,-\varepsilon a)&-\varepsilon b.
\end{array}
\]
Interchanging the first two columns independently changes the sign of their difference. Hence a row
contributes $\varepsilon\mu$ to $C_1+C_2$ and an independently signed value of magnitude $|w|$ to
$C_1-C_2$, with the three pairs $(\mu,|w|)$ displayed above. The overall signs therefore realize
any signing of the $\mu_i$, while the column interchanges independently realize any signing of the
$|w_i|$. Thus $C_1=C_2=0$ if and only if both associated multisets are signable. These modes,
overall signs, and interchanges exhaust all zero-sum orientations of the rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cert}
A group is balanceable if and only if it admits a mode assignment $(\mathrm m_i)_i$ and subsets $U,V$ of indices
with $\sum_{i\in U}\mu_i=\sum_{i\notin U}\mu_i$ and $\sum_{i\in V}|w_i|=\sum_{i\notin V}|w_i|$; the
orientation is then read off constructively.
\end{corollary}

For completeness, put $\nu_i=|w_i|$.  A certificate with signed totals
$\sum_i\varepsilon_i\mu_i=\sum_i\eta_i\nu_i=0$ reconstructs the rows as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-from-certificate}
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_i\mu_i+\eta_i\nu_i}{2},
       \frac{\varepsilon_i\mu_i-\eta_i\nu_i}{2},-\varepsilon_i\mu_i\right).
\end{equation}
In each mode $\mu_i$ and $\nu_i$ have the same parity, so these entries are integers.
The mode formulas show that their absolute values are the original sum-triple.  Formula
\eqref{eq:row-from-certificate} is the decoding rule used by all sign tables below.

The following elementary lemma records the certificate--transport principle used in both
constructions.  Its structural hypotheses must hold throughout the stated range; finite
evaluations alone do not establish them.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:certificate-transport}
Fix positive integers $c,m_0,h$, a parameter path $m_Q=m_0+hQ$, and an integer lower level $Q_0\ge0$,
with $m_Q\ge3$ throughout the stated range.
Suppose that the following data have been specified for every integer $Q\ge Q_0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item A fixed finite list of packet rules constructs $cm_Q$ zero-sum rows,
      grouped into $c$ sets of $m_Q$ rows.  Every row has nonzero, pairwise distinct absolute values.
\item For some fixed positive integer $D$, after fixing coordinate signs and residue classes,
      all absolute entry occurrences in each residue $u\in[1,D]$ are partitioned into labelled packets,
      each enumerated once as a block
      $\{u+Dv:L_i(Q)\le v\le U_i(Q)\}$ with integer-affine endpoints.
      The blocks are disjoint by the endpoint identities
      \[
       L_1=0,\qquad L_{i+1}=U_i+1,\qquad
       U_{\rm last}=\left\lfloor\frac{3cm_Q-u}{D}\right\rfloor.
      \]
      Their order is fixed and each block length is nonnegative; zero length means an empty block.
\item A mode and two signs are assigned to every row. For each group and each of its two
      scalar chains, the row contributions are partitioned into a fixed finite list of labelled
      scalar packets. Each packet belongs to that group and chain independently of $Q$, and
      its mode and relevant sign are fixed. After any absolute-value or sorting breakpoint has
      been split off, each unsigned scalar contribution is
      $aQ+bv+d$ over an integer interval $L(Q)\le v\le U(Q)$ with affine endpoints.
      Every such scalar packet has nonnegative count $U(Q)-L(Q)+1$, with zero count
      interpreted as an empty packet.
\end{enumerate}
If both signed totals in every group vanish at $Q_0,Q_0+1,Q_0+2$, the rule gives an
$\IHS(m_Q,3;c)$ for every $Q\ge Q_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Condition \textup{(ii)} gives exact support by partitioning every residue class into consecutive
quotient intervals.  In \textup{(iii)}, put $N(Q)=U(Q)-L(Q)+1$.  The packet sum is
\[
 N(Q)(aQ+d)+\frac b2N(Q)(L(Q)+U(Q)),
\]
a polynomial of degree at most two.  This formula also gives zero when $N(Q)=0$.
The fixed group and chain labels in \textup{(iii)} therefore make each signed group total
a polynomial of degree at most two. Three consecutive zeros force that polynomial to vanish identically.
Corollary~\ref{cor:cert} gives zero column sums; \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)} give all remaining
IHS conditions.  Any finite values below $Q_0$ must be checked separately.
\end{proof}

\section{Kotzig lift and periodic support correction}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{The half-column lift}

Let $n=3m$ and $r=2n+1$. Zhou--Li--Zhang--Su~\cite[Theorem~2.2]{Zhou2018DM} give the $3\times r$
centre-complementary Kotzig array $A=(a_{i,j})$, $i\in[-1,1]$, $j\in[-n,n]$, by the closed formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kotzig}
 a_{i,j}=\frac{1-(-1)^{\,j+n}\,r}{4}\,i+\frac{2-3|i|}{2}\,j ,
\end{equation}
For odd $n$, this formula is integer-valued; each row is a permutation of $[-n,n]$, each column sums
to zero, and $a_{-i,-j}=-a_{i,j}$. For
$j\in[1,n]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vals}
(a_{-1,j},a_{1,j})=
\begin{cases}
\bigl(\tfrac{n-j}{2},\ -\tfrac{n+j}{2}\bigr), & j\equiv n\pmod2,\\[3pt]
\bigl(-\tfrac{n+1+j}{2},\ \tfrac{n+1-j}{2}\bigr), & j\not\equiv n\pmod2 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lift}
Let $n$ be odd. For $j\in[1,n]$ set
$R_j=\bigl(3a_{-1,j}-1,\ 3j,\ 3a_{1,j}+1\bigr)$. Then each $R_j$ is a zero-sum row whose absolute
values form a sum-triple, the $3n$ absolute values are pairwise distinct, and their union is
\[
 [1,3n+1]\setminus\{h_n\},\qquad h_n=\frac{3n+5}{2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $n=2\kappa-1$. Row sums vanish because each column of $A$ sums to zero and
$-1+0+1=0$. For an odd index $j=2\ell-1$, $1\le\ell\le\kappa$, put
$p=\kappa-\ell$ and $q=\kappa+\ell-1$. By \eqref{eq:vals}, the absolute values are
$\{|3p-1|,3j,3q-1\}$. When $p=0$, one has $q=j=n$ and $1+(3q-1)=3j$; otherwise
$(3p-1)+3j=3q-1$. These rows supply $1$ and all positive values congruent to $2$ modulo $3$ in
$[1,3n+1]$. For an even index $j=2\ell$, $1\le\ell\le\kappa-1$, put
$s=\kappa-\ell$ and $w=\kappa+\ell$. The absolute values are
$\{3s+1,3j,3w+1\}$ and $(3s+1)+3j=3w+1$; they supply all values congruent to $1$ modulo $3$ except
$3\kappa+1$. Finally, the middle entries supply every positive multiple of $3$ through $3n$.
Consequently all values are distinct and their union is
$[1,3n+1]\setminus\{3\kappa+1\}$, where $3\kappa+1=(3n+5)/2$.
\end{proof}

The fixed offset $(-1,0,1)$ therefore leaves a hole at $h_n$ and an overshoot at $3n+1$.
We replace it by zero-sum offsets in $\{-2,\dots,2\}^3$. These permit a bounded redistribution
between neighbouring support values while preserving every row sum.

\subsection{The uniform correction rule}\label{sec:rule}

\begin{definition}\label{def:rule}
Let $n=3m\ge93$, where $m\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\gcd(m,3)=1$. Define an offset assignment
$\delta:[1,n]\to\{-2,\dots,2\}^3$, with coordinate sum zero at every index, by the following
period-$24$ rule. For the class
$n\equiv9\pmod{24}$ there are two $24$-letter offset words $W_1,W_2$ and constant phases $(6,12)$ with
\[
\delta(j)=
\begin{cases}
W_1[(j-6)\bmod24], & 21\le j\le\kappa-21,\\
W_2[(j-12)\bmod24], & \kappa+21\le j\le n-21,
\end{cases}\qquad \kappa=\tfrac{n+1}2,
\]
completed by three bounded boundary tables (for $j\le20$, $|j-\kappa|\le20$, $j\ge n-20$), one set per
residue of $n$ modulo $48$.  The class $n\equiv21\pmod{24}$ uses phases $(10,13)$ and the rotated
words $\widehat W_1[p]=W_1[p+16\bmod24]$, $\widehat W_2[p]=W_2[p+13\bmod24]$.
The corrected row is
\[
 R_j=(3a_{-1,j}+\delta_{-1}(j),\ 3j+\delta_0(j),\ 3a_{1,j}+\delta_1(j));
\]
thus $\delta(j)$ is the full offset, replacing the fixed offset $(-1,0,1)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:lift}.
The two master words and all four boundary-table sets are printed in
Section~\ref{sec:offset-data}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rule}
Let $m\ge123$ be odd with $m\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\gcd(m,3)=1$, and put $n=3m$. Then the corrected
rows of Definition~\ref{def:rule} partition $[1,3n]$ into $n$ sum-triples.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n=e+24t$, where $e\in\{9,21\}$. Choose the unique representative
$t=t_0+12Q$ with $15\le t_0\le26$ and $Q\ge0$. Since $n=3m$ and $\gcd(m,3)=1$, exclude
$t_0\equiv0\pmod3$ for $e=9$ and $t_0\equiv1\pmod3$ for $e=21$.  This leaves $16$ admissible
states.  In a bulk word write
$j=j_0+24h$.  In a fixed state the sign of each coordinate of the corrected lift is fixed, so its
absolute value is affine in $(Q,h)$; its $h$-coefficient has magnitude $36$ or $72$.  Split a
magnitude-$36$ family according to the parity of $h$.  Every resulting family is then an arithmetic
progression with difference $72$.

Fix a residue $u\in[1,72]$ and consider the values congruent to $u$ modulo $72$, after subtracting
$u$ and dividing by $72$. The bulk progressions and the boundary singletons become integer intervals
whose endpoints are affine in $Q$. Substitution of the two word pairs and four boundary-table sets printed in
Section~\ref{sec:offset-data} gives, in each admissible state and for every $u$, consecutive
intervals beginning at $0$ and ending at
\[
  12Q+\left\lfloor\frac{3n_0-u}{72}\right\rfloor,
  \qquad n_0=e+24t_0.
\]
The same substitution verifies, in each row, that the three absolute values satisfy
$a+b=c$ after sorting. The endpoint offsets at $Q=0$ are given by the displayed boundary strings,
and both coefficients of every affine adjacency agree. All packet lengths have nonnegative
constant and linear coefficients, so empty packets cause no change of order.
Thus the same adjacency holds for every $Q\ge0$, and every integer in $[1,3n]$ occurs once.
These identities can be verified by substituting the printed words and boundary tables
and comparing the constant and linear coefficients of the affine endpoints.
\end{proof}

The rule is finite-state in $m$.  Inside either bulk interval, $\delta(j+24)=\delta(j)$, while the
three boundary tables depend only on $n\bmod48$.  More sharply, equation~\eqref{eq:c3-word-shift}
shows that changing class from $n\equiv9$ to $21$, or conversely, is a half-period shift by $12$ in
both effective bulk layers.  Under the formal step $m\mapsto m+4$ the boundary state follows
$9\to21\to33\to45\to9\pmod{48}$.  Thus parameter growth inserts periodic bulk blocks and updates one
of four boundary states; it is not an append-only operation on the arrays.

\section{Grouping orientation and the \texorpdfstring{$c=3$}{c=3} theorem}\label{sec:proof}

Fix the corrected partition from Lemma~\ref{lem:rule}, and write its triples as
$T_1,\dots,T_n$, where $n=3m$. Group them by their index modulo $3$:
$G_r=\{T_j:j\equiv r\pmod3\}$ for $r=0,1,2$. Each group contains exactly $m$ triples and will
become one $m\times3$ array. Within each group, order the rows by increasing $j$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:orient}
Each group $G_r$ is balanceable by an explicit rule. Orient $T_j$ in the decoupled form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:cert}: in the default mode the sum element $c_j$ occupies the third column as
$-\varepsilon_jc_j$, while a sign $\eta_j$ determines the order of the two summands in the first two
columns. At most one designated row in the $\kappa$-window is switched to mode $a$ or $b$ to correct
parity. The signs $(\varepsilon_j,\eta_j)$ are constant on the bulk zones separated by the four
bounded windows at $1,m,\kappa,n$, after subdivision by $j\bmod24$. There are $24$ printed patterns:
one for each of the eight admissible residues of $n$ modulo $144$ and each of the three groups.
Since the coprimality restriction leaves $16$ admissible pairs $(e,\tau)$, and the eight residues
correspond to $s=15+((\tau-15)\bmod6)$ together with $s+6$, each printed pattern represents two of
the $48$ admissible group states in the proof below; it fixes the
signs of the two mode-dependent chains $\{\mu_j\}$ and $\{|w_j|\}$. The complete patterns are in
Section~\ref{sec:orientation-data}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}, it suffices to exhibit, for each group, a mode assignment making both
chains signable. The decoupled certificate of Corollary~\ref{cor:cert} uses mode $\mathrm c$ in every
row except the single row a stored pattern may switch, which always lies in the $\kappa$-window.
The complete $24$ patterns are printed in
Section~\ref{sec:orientation-data}. To certify the infinite tail, write
$n=e+24(\tau+12Q)$ with $e\in\{9,21\}$, $15\le\tau\le26$, and $Q\ge0$.  The coprimality restriction
leaves $16\cdot3=48$ admissible group states. The stored pattern indexed by
$s=15+((\tau-15)\bmod6)$ is used for both $\tau=s$ and $\tau=s+6$, which explains why the
$24$ printed patterns cover all $48$ states. Put $n_0=e+24\tau$, $m_0=n_0/3$, and
$\kappa_0=(n_0+1)/2$. Then $n=n_0+288Q$, $m=m_0+96Q$, and $\kappa=\kappa_0+144Q$.
In each bulk category write $j=j_*+24h$, where $1\le j_*\le24$
and $j_*\equiv r\pmod3$. This fixes the parity of $j$, the offset, and both signs;
no additional parity layer is needed. Each group has eight allowed values of $j_*$ in each
zone, hence $24$ bulk categories altogether.

The three bulk zones and the slopes of their integer-affine $h$-endpoints are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{zone}&\text{row interval}&\text{slopes of }(L_z,U_z)\\\hline
B_1&[21,m-13]&(0,4)\\
B_2&[m+13,\kappa-21]&(4,6)\\
B_3&[\kappa+21,n-21]&(6,12).
\end{array}
\]
Indeed, taking ceilings and floors after division by $24$ preserves affine endpoints,
because $96$, $144$, and $288$ are multiples of $24$. Thus the counts in a fixed category are
$c_0+4Q$, $c_0+2Q$, and $c_0+6Q$, respectively. Every admissible base parameter has $m_0\ge127$;
the three zones at $Q=0$ contain at least $94$, $31$, and $149$ consecutive indices.
Each allowed residue therefore occurs, and all counts stay positive for $Q\ge0$.

Every bulk row is in mode $c$. Let $D_j$ be the middle coordinate $3j+\delta_0(j)$ minus
the other positive summand in the corrected row. Equation~\eqref{eq:vals} gives
\[
 \mu_j=432Q+36h+\alpha,\qquad D_j=-432Q+108h+\beta,
\]
where $\alpha,\beta$ are constant within the category. The possible real zero of $D_j$ lies
in $[m-8/9,m+11/9]$, since $|\delta_i|\le2$. It is contained in the $m$-window, so
$|w_j|=-D_j$ in $B_1$ and $|w_j|=D_j$ in $B_2,B_3$. No bulk packet needs another split.
The boundary orders can be checked from the same formula. In the windows at $1,m,\kappa$,
\[
 D_j=\begin{cases}
 (9j-3n)/2+\delta_0-\delta_{-1},&j\text{ odd},\\
 (9j-3n-3)/2+\delta_0-\delta_1,&j\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus $D_j\le94-3n/2<0$ in the lower endpoint window and
$D_j\ge3n/4-96>0$ in the $\kappa$-window. At $j=m+q$, $D_j$ is independent of $Q$
and is nonzero for the printed offsets.
The coordinate signs in these windows follow from~\eqref{eq:vals}; their positive outer
summand stays positive because $j\le\kappa+20$ and $n\ge381$.
In the upper endpoint window the short coordinate is constant in $Q$, while the other
two absolute coordinates have the same positive slope. Their order, checked at $Q=0$,
therefore remains fixed. Hence every boundary contribution, including a switched mode
in the $\kappa$-window, is affine in $Q$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-transport} shows that each signed total has degree at most two.
Direct substitution of the
decoded signs gives zero at $Q=0,1,\ldots,6$ for both chains and for both values of $\tau$ represented
by each stored row.  Three levels suffice; the remaining four are redundant checks.
Each signed total is therefore the zero polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bridge}
The same offset and orientation rules produce an $\IHS(m,3;3)$ for every
\[
\mathcal B=\{67,71,79,83,91,95,103,107,115,119\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $m\ge67$ the four orientation windows at $1,m,\kappa,n$ are disjoint. In particular,
$\kappa-m=(m+1)/2\ge34>12+20$. Hence the category rule in
Section~\ref{sec:orientation-data} applies without overlap. For every $m\in\mathcal B$, substitute
$n=3m$ in the offset words and select the stored orientation row prescribed there. The ten resulting
checks are printed in Section~\ref{sec:bridge-data}: the absolute-value set has minimum $1$, maximum
$9m$, and cardinality $9m$, while all three column-sum vectors are zero. Row sums are zero by the
construction, so these data prove the assertion.

The set $\mathcal B$ contains every admissible parameter in $[67,119]$.
The next admissible parameter is $127$, which lies in the range $m\ge123$
of Lemma~\ref{lem:rule}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:mtransport}
The statement below is an observation about the corrected-Kotzig normal form and is
\emph{not} used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; that theorem rests on the exhaustive
coverage of the small values, of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}, and of the uniform rules above.
We record it because it explains how the canonical normal form advances from one admissible
parameter to the next.
Put
\[
 d(m)=\begin{cases}4,&m\equiv1\pmod3,\\8,&m\equiv2\pmod3.\end{cases}
\]
Let $m\ge67$ be admissible. Write $R_j^{(m)}$ and $\delta_j^{(m)}$ for the corrected row and its
offset in the construction above.
For every inherited index $1\le j\le3m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c3-standalone-transport}
 R_j^{(m+d)}=R_j^{(m)}+\frac{9d}{2}(-1)^{j+3m}(1,0,-1)
 +\bigl(\delta_j^{(m+d)}-\delta_j^{(m)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The remaining $3d$ target rows are new, with exactly $d$ new indices in each residue class modulo
$3$. The support and orientation states are the next states in the printed finite tables, so the
canonical constructions follow the single chain
\[
 67\xrightarrow{+4}71\xrightarrow{+8}79\xrightarrow{+4}83
 \xrightarrow{+8}91\xrightarrow{+4}\cdots .
\]
This is a recurrence between the canonical corrected-Kotzig forms, not an extension operator on an
arbitrary presentation of an $\IHS(m,3;3)$; it records transport of the chosen normal form.
\end{remark}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the closed Kotzig formula at $3m$ from the formula at $3(m+d)$ and then add the two offset
vectors; this gives~\eqref{eq:c3-standalone-transport}. The count of new rows follows from
$3(m+d)-3m=3d$, and the residue classes distribute these indices equally. Equation
~\eqref{eq:c3-word-shift} gives the half-period bulk update for the $+4$ step, while the four
boundary tables give the stated boundary-state cycle. For target parameters at most $119$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} certifies support and balance. Every larger admissible target is at least
$127$, where Lemmas~\ref{lem:rule} and~\ref{lem:orient} apply.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete \texorpdfstring{$c=4$}{c=4} slice}\label{sec:c4full}

The even parameter $n=4m$ changes the parity bookkeeping in the lift. The support rule uses
two $16$-letter words and four residue classes. For the balance proof we retain a
$48$-position refinement, giving $48$ support states and $192$ group states.
Define $a_{i,j}$ by the same Kotzig formula~\eqref{eq:kotzig}, now with
$n=4m$, $r=2n+1$, $i\in\{-1,0,1\}$ and $j\in[-n,n]$, and put $\kappa=n/2$. The array is the one
of Lemma~\ref{lem:even-kotzig}, which we record here because it supplies the rows used by
the corrected lift~\eqref{eq:c4lift} below.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:even-kotzig}
For even $n$, formula~\eqref{eq:kotzig} is integer-valued and defines a $3\times(2n+1)$
array whose three rows are permutations of $[-n,n]$, whose columns sum to zero, and which satisfies
$a_{-i,-j}=-a_{i,j}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $i=0$ one has $a_{0,j}=j$.  Write $j=2q$ or $j=2q+1$.  When $j=2q$, the entries in
the rows $i=1$ and $i=-1$ are respectively $-n/2-q$ and $n/2-q$; when $j=2q+1$, they are
$n/2-q$ and $-n/2-q-1$.  As $q$ ranges over the permitted integers, each of the two noncentral
rows therefore contains every element of $[-n,n]$ exactly once.  The two first terms in
~\eqref{eq:kotzig} cancel between $i=1$ and $i=-1$, while the coefficients of $j$ in the three
rows sum to $-1/2+1-1/2=0$.  This proves the column sums and the stated central symmetry; it also
shows explicitly why the half-integral terms for odd $j$ cancel.
\end{proof}

We use the corrected lift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c4lift}
R_j=(3a_{-1,j}+d_{-1}(j),\ 3j+d_0(j),\ 3a_{1,j}+d_1(j)).
\end{equation}
Here $1\le j\le n$, and $d(j)=(d_{-1}(j),d_0(j),d_1(j))\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^3$
has coordinate sum zero. The same offset is denoted by $\delta(j)=d(j)$ in the data tables of
Section~\ref{sec:c4-support-data}.
For $\rho=m\bmod12\in\{1,5,7,11\}$, Section~\ref{sec:c4-support-data} prints the two master
words, their support phases, the balance-indexing phases, and both boundary-table sets.
Group rows by $j-1\bmod4$, ordering the rows within each group by increasing $j$.

For the periodic ranges, fix the state parameters by writing uniquely
$m=m_\rho+12s$, where $s=s_0+12Q$, $s_0\in\{0,\ldots,11\}$, $Q\ge0$, and
\[
(m_1,m_5,m_7,m_{11})=(25,29,31,23).
\]
Put $m_0=m_\rho+12s_0$ and $n_0=4m_0$, so $n=n_0+576Q$.
The $48$ choices of $(\rho,s_0)$ fix all phases, boundary tables, and parities.
Together with the group index $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, they give the $192$ balance states.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:c4-packet-degree}
For a fixed state $(\rho,s_0,r)$ of the $c=4$ construction, every category total of
$\mu$ and $|w|$ is a polynomial in $Q$ of degree at most two for $Q\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $n=n_0+576Q$.  All word positions below are indexed by the balance phases
$(\phi_1,\phi_2)$ of Section~\ref{sec:c4-support-data}, that is $p=(j-\phi_i-16s)\bmod48$, and
not by the support phases $\psi_i$. Since $s=s_0+12Q$, the phase $16s\bmod48$ is independent of $Q$.
The fixed rotation $W_{\rho,i}[p]=P_i[(p+\phi_i-\psi_i)\bmod16]$ re-indexes the same
$16$-letter word. The layer parity $e$ is recorded separately. For a bulk category write
$j=j_*+96h$, where $j_*=j_0+48e$, $1\le j_0\le48$, and $e\in\{0,1\}$.
This fixes the word letter, the layer parity, the group, and both printed signs.
Every bulk row is in mode $c$.
Away from the endpoint windows, its sum element is
\[
 \mu_j=\begin{cases}
 3(n+j)/2-d_1,&j\text{ even},\\
 3(n+j+1)/2-d_{-1},&j\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
\]
and its signed summand difference is $D_j=432h-864Q+B$, so $|w_j|=|D_j|$, where
\[
 B=\begin{cases}
 (9j_*-3n_0)/2+d_0-d_{-1},&j\text{ even},\\
 (9j_*-3(n_0+1))/2+d_0-d_1,&j\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The offset coordinates are constant in a fixed category.  The sign change of this last
expression occurs between
\[
 h\le 2Q+\lfloor-B/432\rfloor
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 h\ge 2Q+\lfloor-B/432\rfloor+1.
\]
In the lower bulk the original endpoints have slopes $0$ and $3$ in $Q$;
in the upper bulk they have slopes $3$ and $6$.  Since $n_0\ge92$,
one has $n\ge668$ at $Q=1$.  The real sign-change position lies in
$[n/3-8/9,n/3+11/9]$, strictly between $21$ and $n/2-21$ on this tail.
Substitution of $21\le j\le n/2-21$ and $|d_i|\le2$ shows that both lower-bulk pieces
have nonnegative counts from that level onward;
the upper bulk lies wholly above the sign change.  Thus these refined endpoints are
integer-affine for every $Q\ge1$. For the boundary rows, the same difference formula gives
$D_j\le94-3n/2<0$ at $j\le20$ and $D_j\ge3n/4-96>0$ at $|j-n/2|\le20$,
since $n\ge668$ on this tail. Their coordinate signs follow from the Kotzig formula.
At $j=n+q$ the short coordinate is independent of $Q$ and the two large absolute
coordinates have the same positive slope, so the order at $Q=1$ persists.
The boundary scalar contributions, including any low-window mode switch, are therefore affine.
Applying the packet-sum formula in Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-transport} proves the assertion.
At $Q=0$ a refined piece may be truncated to empty before its affine formula stabilizes,
which explains the separate base check.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A base-level branch change.}
Take $(\rho,s_0,r)=(11,0,1)$, so $n=92+576Q$. The lower-bulk category with
$p=26$ and $e=0$ consists of $j=26+96h$ with $0\le h\le3Q-1$.
Here $\phi_1=\psi_1=0$, and the offset is $P_1[10]=\texttt{4}$, namely $(0,-1,1)$.

For $Q\ge1$, split at $h=2Q$. The two ranges $[0,2Q]$ and $[2Q+1,3Q-1]$
give $\nu$-totals $864Q^2+476Q+22$ and $216Q^2-238Q+22$, respectively.
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum\mu_j&=3240Q^2+312Q,\\
 \sum\nu_j&=1080Q^2+238Q+44\qquad(Q\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both printed signs for this category are positive. At $Q=0$ the lower bulk is $[21,25]$,
so the category is empty and its actual totals are zero, whereas the latter polynomial gives $44$.
The second branch would have the negative formal count $Q-1=-1$ there, outside the hypotheses
of Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-transport}. This is why the base level is checked separately.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:c4full}]
Every row in~\eqref{eq:c4lift} sums to zero: the three Kotzig entries in a column sum to zero,
and every displayed offset vector has coordinate sum zero. It remains to verify support and column
balance.
In a bulk position $j=j_0+48h$, every absolute coordinate of~\eqref{eq:c4lift} is affine in
$(Q,h)$, with $h$-coefficient $\pm72$ or $\pm144$. Splitting the former by layer parity gives
progressions of difference $144$. For each residue $u\in\{1,\ldots,144\}$, subtracting $u$
and dividing by $144$ turns the decoded values into integer intervals. The printed words and
boundary tables make these intervals consecutive, beginning at $0$ and ending at
$12Q+\lfloor(3n_0-u)/144\rfloor$. Both coefficients of each affine adjacency agree, and all
packet lengths have nonnegative constant and linear coefficients. Thus these finite endpoint
identities give the support $[1,3n]$ for every $Q\ge0$, allowing empty packets at $Q=0$.
The same substitution verifies that the sorted absolute values in every row satisfy $a+b=c$.

For example, in state $(\rho,s_0)=(1,0)$ and residue $u=138$, the quotient intervals are
\[
 [0,3Q-1],\quad [3Q,6Q-1],\quad \{6Q\},\quad
 [6Q+1,9Q],\quad [9Q+1,12Q],\quad \{12Q+1\}.
\]
Their lengths are $3Q,3Q,1,3Q,3Q,1$, and they partition $[0,12Q+1]$.
The first interval comes from coordinate $i=-1$ in the upper bulk at
$j=8+96h$, $3Q+1\le h\le6Q$. Its offset is $(0,-2,2)$, and its absolute value
$138+864Q-144h$ has quotient $6Q-h$. The singleton $\{6Q\}$ comes from coordinate $i=1$
at $j=9$, whose absolute value is $138+864Q$.
At $Q=0$ the four bulk packets are empty; the two boundary singletons give the values $138$ and $282$.

For balance, Appendix~\ref{sec:c4-balance-data-appendix} prints one mode-and-two-sign row for each
of the $192$ group states. Lemma~\ref{lem:c4-packet-degree} establishes the degree bound for
$Q\ge1$. The printed signs give zero at $Q=1,\ldots,6$, so the first three levels already force
both signed totals to vanish identically on this tail. The other three are redundant checks.
The separate $Q=0$ tally in the appendix gives all $2\cdot192=384$ base equations;
this level is not used in the interpolation argument.
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} converts the two sign identities into zero column sums.
The periodic ranges start at $m=25,29,31,23$ in the four residue classes. The only smaller
admissible values are $5,7,11,13,17,19$, and Section~\ref{sec:c4-small-data} gives complete compact
constructions for all six. This covers every admissible $m$.
\end{proof}

\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:remarks}

Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:c4full} settle the $c=3$ and $c=4$ slices of
Problem~\ref{prob:1}. In both cases, the essential requirement is to make an exact support
partition compatible with several separately balanced arrays. The corrected Kotzig lift,
the congruence-based grouping, and the two sign identities provide this compatibility.
Explicit small constructions complete the ranges below the uniform rules.
The same centre-complementary viewpoint appears in magic three-dimensional
rectangles~\cite{Zhou2018JCD}; here it supplies the starting array, while the offset corrections
produce the required absolute-value support.

Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-transport} gives the common verification principle. Once coordinate
signs, residue classes, and absolute-value branches are fixed, support reduces to affine
endpoint identities and balance reduces to polynomial identities of degree at most two.
The separate $Q=0$ check in the $c=4$ construction illustrates why branch stabilization must
precede polynomial interpolation. Applying this principle to another slice requires new
support and balance certificates satisfying the lemma's hypotheses.

\paragraph{Code and data availability.}
All finite construction data required for the proofs are included in Sections~\ref{sec:certificates},
\ref{sec:small-data}, and~\ref{sec:c4-data}, and Appendix~\ref{sec:c4-balance-data-appendix}.
The printed certificates suffice to reconstruct the arrays and verify their exact support
and zero row and column sums, independently of computer code.
The reproducibility package is available at
\href{https://github.com/matheorist/heffter-width3-c34}{\texttt{matheorist/heffter-width3-c34}}
for computational verification.
