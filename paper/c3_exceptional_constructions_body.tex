\section{Three exceptional supports and six preperiod orientations}\label{sec:small-data}
This appendix supplies the nine finite cases below the periodic tail that are not covered by the
displayed $m=7$ example.  The data are compressed but lossless: the parameter $m$, one offset
word (when needed), one switch, and two hexadecimal sign strings reconstruct every signed entry.
Only $m=11,19,23$ require exceptional support words.  The six values
$m=31,35,43,47,55,59$ already use the uniform support rule, but precede the disjoint-window range of
the periodic orientation theorem and therefore retain separate orientation certificates.  Three
support words and $27$ orientation records replace an explicit listing of all
$969=3(11+19+23+31+35+43+47+55+59)$ rows.

Put \(n=3m\), and let \(a_{i,j}\) be the Kotzig entry defined in
Section~\ref{sec:construction}.  For an offset \(\delta_j=(d_{1j},d_{2j},d_{3j})\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_j=(a_j,b_j,c_j)&:=\operatorname{sort}\{\,|3a_{-1,j}+d_{1j}|,\ |3j+d_{2j}|,\\
&\qquad |3a_{1,j}+d_{3j}|\,\},\\
0&<a_j<b_j<c_j,\qquad a_j+b_j=c_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(m=31,35,43,47,55,59\), the offsets are exactly those given by the rule in
Section~\ref{sec:offset-data}.
Direct substitution gives the partition of \([1,9m]\) in all six cases; no new support word is
needed.  For the three smaller parameters, use the following radius-two alphabet and words.
Every listed offset has coordinate sum zero, and each word has length $n=3m$.

Thus the support part of each exceptional construction is checked independently of the
orientation strings.  It remains to orient the triples.  For \(r=0,1,2\), order the group as
\(G_r=(T_{3t+r+1})_{t=0}^{m-1}\), and write
\(T_{3t+r+1}=(a_{r,t},b_{r,t},c_{r,t})\) for the ordered
entries of its \(t\)-th triple.  The default mode is \(c\); an entry \(t:a\) or
\(t:b\) in the switch column changes only row \(t\) to that mode.  For a row in mode
\(c,a,b\), respectively, define
\[
(\mu_{r,t},\nu_{r,t},x_{r,t})=
(c_{r,t},b_{r,t}-a_{r,t},c_{r,t}),\quad
(a_{r,t},b_{r,t}+c_{r,t},a_{r,t}),\quad
(b_{r,t},a_{r,t}+c_{r,t},b_{r,t}).
\]
Expand each hexadecimal word into binary, retain exactly its first \(m\) bits (including any
leading zeroes), and read \(1\) as \(+1\) and \(0\) as \(-1\).  Let the words \(E,H\) give the signs
\(\varepsilon_{r,t},\eta_{r,t}\), respectively.  The reconstructed row is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptional-row}
R_{r,t}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{r,t}\mu_{r,t}+\eta_{r,t}\nu_{r,t}}{2},
\frac{\varepsilon_{r,t}\mu_{r,t}-\eta_{r,t}\nu_{r,t}}{2},-\varepsilon_{r,t}x_{r,t}\right).
\end{equation}

For every $(m,r)$ row of the table, direct decoding of the two sign strings gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\varepsilon_{r,t}\mu_{r,t}=0,
\qquad \sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\eta_{r,t}\nu_{r,t}=0.
\]
Because $x_{r,t}=\mu_{r,t}$ in all three modes, the first identity also controls the
third column.  Equation~\eqref{eq:exceptional-row} therefore has zero row sums and each
of its three column sums is zero.  The three exceptional support words are audited above;
for $m=31,35,43,47,55,59$, the support is supplied by the uniform rule in
Section~\ref{sec:offset-data}.  Thus the displayed words and table are complete,
independently checkable constructions for all nine preperiod values.
